1. Master's Thesis

Detecting Dark Matter Substructure in Galaxies

Ruyu Li
M.Sc. Machine Learning in Science, University of Nottingham, August 2021

This thesis applies simulation-based inference (SBI) to constrain the dark matter subhalo mass function from strong gravitational lensing images. By pairing a CNN embedding with neural ratio estimation, the pipeline recovers two key population parameters — the substructure mass fraction (fsub) and the power-law slope (β) — from simulated single-band images consistent with the Euclid space telescope. A lightweight embedding network (SimpleNet) achieves a mean absolute error of 0.005, outperforming direct CNN regression by more than an order of magnitude. The thesis also systematically evaluates the impact of observational nuisance parameters — including lens redshift, source position, PSF convolution, shot noise, pixel resolution, and lens ellipticity — on posterior quality.

2. Peer-Reviewed Publication

Exploring the Upper Limits of Text-Based Collaborative Filtering Using Large Language Models: Discoveries and Insights

Ruyu Li, Wenhao Deng, Yu Cheng, Zheng Yuan, Jiaqi Zhang, Fajie Yuan
Published at CIKM 2025 (ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management)

This paper investigates the potential of large language models (LLMs) as item encoders in text-based collaborative filtering (TCF) for recommender systems. We conduct a systematic study across LLMs of varying sizes (125M to 175B parameters), comparing frozen versus fine-tuned representations on three diverse recommendation datasets (MIND, H&M, and Bili). Our results demonstrate that frozen LLM representations significantly outperform traditional ID-based collaborative filtering, with performance scaling correlating to model size. The work provides both theoretical insights and practical guidelines for deploying LLM-based recommendation systems.

My Contribution: I designed and conducted all experiments, implemented the TCF framework including block-wise inference for large-scale LLM embedding extraction, performed the analysis across all three datasets, and wrote the manuscript.

Evidence of Quality: CIKM is a top-tier venue in information retrieval and knowledge management, ranked as a CCF-B conference. The paper is indexed in the ACM Digital Library.

3. Technical Report

Meta Engine: A Framework for Integrating Heterogeneous LLM-Based Query Systems for Multi-Modal Data

Ruyu Li, Tinghui Zhang, Haodi Ma, Daisy Zhe Wang, Yifan Wang
arXiv preprint, February 2026  |  arXiv:2602.01701

This technical report presents Meta Engine, a unified framework that integrates heterogeneous LLM-based query systems for multi-modal data analytics. As semantic query capabilities expand across text, images, tables, and structured data, the current landscape is fragmented — each modality has its own specialized query engine with incompatible interfaces. Meta Engine addresses this by introducing a “query system on query systems” architecture comprising five components: a Natural Language Query Parser, Operator Generator, Query Router, modality-specific Adapters, and a Result Aggregator. Experiments show that Meta Engine achieves 3–6× higher F1 scores in most cases compared to single-engine baselines.

My Contribution: I implemented the core framework including the query parser and routing components, designed and conducted the experiments, and led the writing of the paper.

Faculty Supervision: This work was conducted under the supervision of Prof. Yifan Wang (University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa).
